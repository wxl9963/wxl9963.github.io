Portal annular pancreas (PAP) is a rare congenital anatomical variation in which pancreatic parenchyma encircles the portal vein or superior mesenteric vein. We report a 55-year-old male with obstructive jaundice caused by distal cholangiocarcinoma, concurrent with complete PAP (Joseph Type IIIb) and a common hepatic artery originating from the superior mesenteric artery (Michels type IX). Preoperative multiphasic contrast-enhanced CT with three-dimensional vascular reconstruction delineated the complex vascular and ductal anatomy. A classical pancreaticoduodenectomy was performed using a phased pancreatic transection strategy with en bloc resection of the retroportal pancreatic tissue, preserving the aberrant hepatic artery. The postoperative course was uneventful apart from a biochemical leak, with no clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula. This case highlights the importance of detailed preoperative imaging and individualized, anatomy-oriented surgical planning in complex pancreatic surgery.

**Keywords:** aberrant hepatic artery, anatomical variation, distal cholangiocarcinoma, pancreaticoduodenectomy, portal annular pancreas
